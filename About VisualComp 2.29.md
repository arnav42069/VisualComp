## Version 4 — Full (≈1000 words)

# VisualComp 2: Compression You Can Finally See

## The problem with compressors

Every engineer remembers learning compression the hard way. You turn the threshold knob, something changes — maybe — and you spend years developing the ear to know what attack and release are really doing to your transients. Traditional compressor plugins give you a gain reduction meter, a handful of knobs, and no real insight into what's happening to your actual waveform.

VisualComp 2.29 was built on a simple idea: **if you can see compression working, you can master it** — whether you're a first-year producer or a veteran engineer who wants to work faster.

## Twin real-time waveform displays

At the heart of VisualComp 2.29 are two oscilloscope-style displays showing roughly 1.3 seconds of scrolling audio: your **input signal** on the left, your **compressed output** on the right. The threshold is drawn as a fine dashed line directly across the input waveform, so you can see exactly which peaks are crossing it — and by how much. On the output side, you watch those same peaks come back controlled, rounded and glued.

The result is immediate, intuitive feedback. Set your attack too slow? You'll *see* the transient sneak through. Release too fast? You'll *see* the pumping. It's compression tuition and mixing tool in one.

## Four circuits, one rack

Hardware compressors have personalities, and VisualComp 2.29 models four of the most loved:

- **VCA** — Clean, fast, symmetrical. The bus compressor sound: punchy drums, tight mixes, transparent glue. When you want control without color, this is your circuit.
- **FET** — Modelled on the behavior of classic field-effect-transistor units, with lightning attack times down to fractions of a millisecond and a musical saturation that adds bite. Drums, bass, rap vocals, parallel crush.
- **Optical** — A photocell-style gain element with program-dependent, two-stage release. Smooth, forgiving, and famously musical on vocals, acoustic guitar and bass. It doesn't grab — it leans.
- **Tube** — Variable-mu behavior where the ratio itself deepens with drive, plus gentle harmonic warmth. Lead vocals, mix bus, mastering.

Each mode reshapes the compressor's ballistics and adds its own saturation character, so switching circuits genuinely changes how the plugin feels — not just a label on a menu.

## Sidechain compression — visualized

VisualComp 2.29 accepts an external sidechain input, and this is where the visual philosophy pays off most. Enable sidechain mode and a **live ducking overlay** appears on the input display: a bright ceiling line that pushes down into the waveform exactly when — and exactly as much as — the key signal drives the compressor. The overlay responds to your threshold, ratio, knee, attack and release in real time, so you can dial a kick-driven bass duck or an EDM pump *by eye* and then refine by ear.

## A complete channel strip of dynamics tools

- **Threshold, ratio, knee, attack, release** — full manual control with vintage-style knobs, graduated tick rings and precise value read-outs
- **Input and output gain faders** — ±24 dB staging on both sides
- **Auto Gain** — continuously measures the level lost to compression and applies matching makeup gain, so you can compare compressed and bypassed signal at equal loudness
- **Mix control** — instant parallel (New York) compression from a single knob in the header
- **0 dB safety limiter** — one click keeps your output ceiling honest
- **Analog GR needle meter** — a vintage-style gain reduction meter with ballistic needle movement and an activity lamp, scaled linearly so the needle always points at the truth
- **Bypass** — instant, glowing-red, true pass-through
- **Zoom** — 50 % to 200 % interface scaling, remembered per instance

## 34 presets built on studio history

The factory library wasn't guessed — it was researched against decades of documented practice with the SSL G-Series bus compressor, FET and optical studio units, and modern mastering workflows. Categories cover:

- **Mastering & Mix Bus** — from the default *Mastering Glue* (a transparent 2:1, 30 ms/100 ms setting that gels a mix without squashing it) to warm tube masters and loudness-forward finishing
- **Drums** — bus glue, kick punch, snare crack, parallel smash, room crush, overhead taming
- **Vocals** — smooth optical leveling, upfront FET presence, tube thickening, rap vocal aggression, whisper rescue
- **Bass** — solid FET bass guitar, flat synth bass, upright warmth, 808 control
- **Guitar & Keys** — acoustic evening, funk snap, piano glue, pad smoothing
- **Utility & FX** — EDM sidechain pump, podcast leveling, parallel thickener, brickwall safety

Every preset sets all nine parameters plus the circuit mode, so it's a complete starting point — not a vague suggestion. And when you've shaped your own sound, **save it**: user presets live in your Documents folder, appear right in the preset browser, and travel with you between sessions and machines.

## Vintage outside, modern inside

The interface is a piece of hardware you'd want in your rack: a black metallic faceplate with subtle wear, machined knobs lit by orange value arcs, an analog needle meter, and glowing push buttons — all rendered in crisp resolution-independent vector graphics that stay sharp at any zoom level, on any display.

## Under the hood

- Sample-accurate envelope detection with per-circuit ballistics
- Soft-knee compression curve with fully variable knee width
- Lock-free visualization pipeline — the audio thread never waits on the interface
- Automatic gain compensation measured pre-makeup to avoid feedback artifacts
- Full parameter automation in any VST3 host
- State saving: your settings, circuit mode, preset name and zoom persist in your DAW project
